    Recording and evaluating RL policies (humanlike.rl)¶

    pip install -e ".[rl]" (gymnasium, mujoco; .[minari] adds the official minari package).

    MuJoCo / Gymnasium environments¶

    from humanlike.rl import HumanlikeRecorder
env = HumanlikeRecorder(make_env(), body_map="humenv-smpl")
obs, info = env.reset(options={"humanlike": {"group": "1.10", "commanded_speed": 1.1, "skip_s": 10}})
while True:
    obs, rew, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(policy(obs))   # info["humanlike"]: this step's samples
    if terminated or truncated:
        break
ds = env.dataset("MyPolicy")       # humanlike.engine.evaluate(ds), or saved as a Minari dataset

    The recorder samples after every physics step, not every env step: while the wrapped env steps, mujoco.mj_step is intercepted for this env's MjData and run one step at a time. The trajectory is unchanged (tests/test_rl.py) and the samples are the profile signals (PROFILE.md):

    signal from
    pelvis, left/right foot position xpos of the body map's bodies
    left/right ground reaction force contact forces between the floor and each foot's bodies (mj_contactForce, world frame, force on the foot)
    centre of mass position, velocity subtree_com, subtree_linvel of the root
    joint torque, velocity qfrc_actuator, qvel of every DOF except the free root
    sagittal angle, moment, power the body map's hip, knee, ankle (deg, N m, W; sign per the body map)

    The number of physics steps per env step (k) is read from frame_skip or given as physics_steps=; it must be constant. Episodes whose pelvis drops below fell_height (0.5 m), or that end by termination, are marked as falls. An environment that steps without mujoco.mj_step (bound by name at import, or stepped in compiled code) raises an error at the first step; allow_env_rate=True records one sample per env step instead (too coarse for the GRF analyses below ~100 Hz).

    Body map. Where the pelvis, feet and sagittal joints are: a preset (humenv-smpl, myoleg) or BodyMap(pelvis, left_foot, right_foot, sagittal={"hip": (joint, sign), ...}, side, leg_length_m). The sign makes flexion positive at the hip and knee and dorsiflexion positive at the ankle. The floor is a geom name, several names (terrain of several geoms), or "world" (every geom not part of the robot).

    Check the recording first. env.check() tests a finished episode (humanlike.rl.checks); evaluate_policy does this after the first episode that did not fall and stops on a failure:

    check fails when
    horizontal GRF the pelvis travels (≥ 0.3 m/s) but the largest horizontal floor force is < 3 % of body weight (normal forces only, no friction), or the mean horizontal force differs from m Δv / T by > 2 % of body weight
    force vs COM accel. at every sample the floor force should equal m (a_COM + g): per axis slope outside 0.9–1.1 or r < 0.8 (wrong force scale, missed contacts)
    sample rate below 100 Hz (physics steps not captured)
    vertical GRF Fz below -2 % of body weight (force of the foot on the floor, or z not up)
    impulse balance mean vertical floor force differs from m (g + Δv_z / T) by more than 10 % (missed floor contacts)
    unlisted contacts the feet touch geoms outside the robot that are not the floor (terrain missing from floor)
    non-foot contacts other bodies carry ≥ 5 % of the vertical floor force (a foot segment missing from the feet)
    feet below pelvis median pelvis height above the feet < 0.3 leg lengths (wrong bodies or up axis)
    joint axes (MuJoCo) a sagittal joint does not turn about the mediolateral axis L = z × forward (|axis · L| < 0.5: an abduction or rotation joint), or its sign disagrees with the direction of the turn (hip flexion and ankle dorsiflexion turn about −L, knee flexion about +L)
    segment rotation the recorded angle does not follow the rotation of the distal segment relative to the proximal one (pelvis–thigh, thigh–shank, shank–foot) about the same axes: r < 0.5 (r ≤ −0.5: flipped sign), or slope outside 0.3–3 (radians vs degrees)
    knee sign, hip sign only without segment orientations: median knee < −5 deg, median hip < −15 deg (gross errors only)

    Forward is where the feet point (MuJoCo), else where the pelvis travelled. On the BFM-TS rollout the signs pass (segment rotation r = 0.86, 0.80, 0.79) and flipping any one of hip, knee or ankle fails both sign checks.

    Minari. minari_io.collector(env) records the same infos with a Minari DataCollector, and minari_io.finish(collector, "org/name-v0", episodes=env.episode_metadata(), **env.profile_metadata()) writes the dataset with the profile metadata.

    Evaluation protocol¶

    from humanlike.rl import evaluate_policy
out = evaluate_policy(make_env, policy, "humenv-smpl", "locomotion-speed-v1")
out["results"]; out["dataset"]

    make_env(speed) returns an environment commanded to move at speed m/s; policy(obs) returns an action (a reset() method is called at every episode). The protocol fixes what makes models comparable:

    protocol speeds (m/s) rollouts per speed duration left out
    locomotion-speed-v1 0.5-4.3, step 0.2 20 20 s first 10 s
    locomotion-speed-walk-v1 0.5-1.9, step 0.2 20 20 s first 10 s
    locomotion-speed-quick-v1 0.7, 1.1, 1.5 3 15 s first 5 s (development only)

    locomotion-speed-v1 is the protocol of the manuscript's rollouts.

    The protocols are for level ground. The analyses and the human references assume it: z is up, the floor is horizontal, speeds come from the horizontal pelvis motion, and gait events, vertical forces and the pendular energy exchange are read against gravity. Slopes, stairs and uneven terrain are outside the protocols, and the recording checks fail a steady climb or descent ("level ground").

    humanlike validate DATA --protocol locomotion-speed-v1 (humanlike.rl.conformance) checks that a data set was recorded under a protocol, as the leaderboard does for a submission: every commanded speed with its rollouts (a fallen rollout counts towards its speed and may be shorter; the falls are reported), the duration of the others within one 30 Hz step, the transient left out, sampling at >= 100 Hz, and the recording checks on one rollout at the slowest, middle and fastest speed. humanlike summarize results.json gives the leaderboard row (humanlike.summary): the headline number of each analysis against the public human reference, never combined into one score.

    Memory, provenance and a first look¶

    Memory - A protocol at the physics rate is large: 400 rollouts of 20 s at 500 Hz with the torques of a 23-joint humanoid take ~3 GB in float64. - Both recorders take three options (humanlike.rl.storage), applied when an episode ends: - dtype="float32" halves the memory. - keep_transient=False drops the start-up transient while recording, half again for the protocols. The rollouts record it as transient_dropped_s, which the protocol conformance counts. - spill_dir= writes each episode to .npy files and keeps memory-mapped views only. - evaluate_policy(..., recorder_kw={"dtype": "float32", "keep_transient": False}) passes them on. - rec.memory_bytes() reports what the finished episodes hold in memory.

    Provenance. evaluate_policy stores what produced the data in the dataset metadata (dataset.provenance), and it is kept in the Minari profile metadata: - the protocol and the seed (episode i uses seed + i); - the body map and the recording options; - the versions of humanlike, Python, NumPy and the simulator packages; - the names of make_env and the policy; - the time of recording.

    It is enough to rerun the protocol with the same code; it does not replace the policy's own checkpoint and configuration.

    humanlike inspect DATA gives a quick overview without running the analyses: - the rollouts, falls, commanded and measured speeds; - the sampling rate and the transient; - the signals, the foot-strike source and the provenance; - for each analysis, whether the data's speeds, rollouts and strides meet its minimum and recommended coverage (docs/oss/COVERAGE.md).

    MyoLeg (KINESIS)¶

    The myoleg body map was checked on the manuscript's KINESIS rollouts (scripts/check_rl_myoleg.py): the physics-rate logs replayed state by state through the recorder's extractor reproduce the logged ground reaction forces, and every recording check passes after the start-up. The check covers joint axes +1 for hip, knee and ankle, and segment rotation r 0.98–1.00. The feet calcn + toes carry the whole floor force.

    During its first ~3 s KINESIS stands and turns. Joint axes averaged over that part, against the first sample's forward direction, do not match the gait. This is why check() leaves out each episode's skip_s.

    Isaac Lab¶

    from humanlike.rl.isaaclab import IsaacLabRecorder, add_foot_contact_sensors
add_foot_contact_sensors(env_cfg, body_map)          # before gym.make: one ground-filtered sensor per foot body
env = gym.make(task, cfg=env_cfg)
rec = IsaacLabRecorder(env, body_map)
for _ in range(n):
    obs, rew, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(policy(obs))
    rec.after_step(terminated, truncated)
rec.close(); ds = rec.dataset("MyPolicy")

    The recorder wraps scene.update (called after every physics step) and samples all environments: body_pos_w, the foot GRFs, the mass-weighted body centres of mass, the joint torques (below), joint_vel, joint_pos.

    Ground reaction forces need the foot sensors. An Isaac Lab contact sensor's net_forces_w sums the normal contact forces only, so the scene's usual contact_forces sensor gives no braking or propulsive force (in the H1 velocity task its horizontal component is ~1e-5 N). add_foot_contact_sensors adds one sensor per foot body, filtered to the ground (Isaac Lab reports filtered contacts only for a sensor on a single body); the recorder adds their normal forces (force_matrix_w) and friction forces (friction_forces_w where Isaac Lab has track_friction_forces, else the PhysX contact view's get_friction_data, e.g. Isaac Lab 2.3). Without these sensors the recorder falls back to net_forces_w, warns and labels the data set (grf_source), and check() fails on walking data. The ground defaults to the flat terrain's collision plane; pass ground= for other terrain.

    rec.check() runs the checks above (without the contact checks only MuJoCo can make, and without the joint-axis check). For the segment-rotation check give the body map's segments (pelvis, thigh, shank and foot bodies); their body_quat_w is sampled at ~50 Hz for the check only.

    Joint torques of implicit actuators come from PhysX. With implicit actuators (ImplicitActuator, PD gains handed to PhysX; the default of many Isaac Lab humanoids, H1 among them), applied_torque is Isaac Lab's evaluation of the PD law on the state before the step. PhysX applies the drive implicitly, so that is not the effort it applies.

    The recorder takes the drive torque from PhysX instead (joint_torque="auto"): the joint force PhysX transmits along each DOF (root_physx_view.get_dof_projected_joint_forces()) plus the joint armature times the DOF acceleration. The armature (a rotor inertia) is needed because PhysX's mass matrix leaves it out.

    scripts/check_isaaclab_torques.py established this on the H1 task with its published policy:

    Test Reference Result
    pelvis fixed in the air, explicit actuators the applied torque M·q̈ + c + g from PhysX's own dynamics terms, plus armature·q̈, reproduces it away from joint limits: R² ≥ 0.997, summed |power| within 0.03 %
    walking, explicit actuators the applied torque projected joint force + armature·q̈: R² 0.998–0.9999 per joint group, |power| within 0.1 %
    walking, implicit actuators projected joint force + armature·q̈ applied_torque is off by +4.9 % in summed |power| (knee R² 0.996); the PD law on the post-step state by −0.9 % (ankle R² 0.84)

    Caveats - At a joint limit the transmitted force also holds the passive limit force. There (within 0.01 rad of a limit) the recorder uses applied_torque, and check() reports the share of such samples ("joint limits": fails above 5 %). - joint_torque="applied" keeps the old behaviour; "physx" uses the PhysX torque for explicit actuators too. - Without the PhysX articulation view the recorder falls back to applied_torque and warns.

    The package runs with Isaac Sim's numpy (1.26) as well as numpy 2.

    Validation¶
    MuJoCo recorder vs the manuscript's BFM-TS rollouts identical to float32 precision (relative difference < 1e-7) for GRF, COM, torques, velocities, positions; k = 15, 450 Hz; passes every check, and each flipped sagittal sign fails (scripts/check_rl_recorder_bfm.py)
    checks each failure produced on purpose (wrong foot body, terrain geom missing from the floor, flipped knee, uncaptured physics steps) in tests/test_rl.py
    trajectory unchanged by recording, GRF = body weight when standing, Minari round trip, protocol tests/test_rl.py (small MuJoCo model)
    Isaac Lab recorder Isaac Lab 2.3 / Isaac Sim 5.1, H1 velocity task with its published policy at 1 m/s, GPU and CPU (scripts/check_rl_recorder_isaaclab.py): every check passes (force vs COM acceleration slope 1.00, r 0.95-0.99 per axis; segment rotation r 1.00), each flipped sagittal sign fails, the normal-only fallback fails the horizontal-GRF and force checks; the engine runs on the recording (the kinetic stage skips: the policy's contacts last ~0.09 s, below the 0.10 s a strike needs)
    Isaac Lab recorder, interface against an object with Isaac Lab's interface (tests/test_rl.py)
    myoleg preset bodies, joints and floor found in KINESIS's myolegs.xml; joint axes give the preset's signs (+1, +1, +1); not yet run in a rollout
