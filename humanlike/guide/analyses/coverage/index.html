    How much data each analysis needs¶

    reports/coverage_sensitivity.py subsamples the manuscript's rollouts. The full design is 20 commanded speeds × 20 rollouts (KINESIS: 10 speeds). Each draw keeps either k random commanded speeds (with all their rollouts) or r random rollouts at every speed. Each analysis is then recomputed exactly as reported. The full-data values reproduce the manuscript: sagittal 2/3/7/1/2, principles 7/10/10/5/10, AMP FPGD 180.9, and the foot-placement refits are identical to the curated fits.

    The study used 200 draws per level (50 for the principles and 20 for the foot-placement refits). The output files are in reports/output/coverage/: summary.csv, ranking.csv and draws.csv.

    Findings (2026-10-25)¶

    Analysis Speeds needed Rollouts per speed What goes wrong below
    JS stride length / frequency ≥ 6 in the reference range (≤ 10 % from full for every model) 1–2 suffice (≤ 16 %) The speed distribution enters the joint KDE. With 1 speed, AMP is 75 % off. The ranking of close models (BFM-TS 0.80, KINESIS 0.82) needs every speed. BFM-TA is at the 1-bit ceiling.
    FPGD 1–4 (≤ 10 %; groups are scored independently) ≥ 10 (≈ 100 strides per group fit) Biased upward with few strides. At r = 2: AMP 231 vs 181, KINESIS 384 vs 318, SMP 205 vs 185. Small submissions look less human-like. AMP vs SMP (2 % apart) cannot be ranked from any subsample.
    Peak ΔR² BFM: 12 speeds still 6–13 % off ≥ 5–10 Depends on which speeds are included (strongly for BFM): the mean over speed groups mixes speeds.
    CoT verdict the full range, walking and running (≥ 12) ≥ 10 Spurious PASS. With 4 speeds, AMP gets PASS in 68 % of draws (all models FAIL with full data). With 1 speed the fit fails and is labelled FAIL (should be "not evaluable").
    Sagittal (features with |median z| ≤ 2) ≥ 4 for ±1 feature ≥ 3 for ±1 Exact counts flip near the threshold (AMP ankle median z −2.05). At r = 10, AMP agrees in 57 % of draws and KINESIS in 78 %.
    Kinetic principles ≥ 3 spanning ≥ 0.6 m/s for all 16 to be evaluable; ≥ 6 for ±1 ≥ 3–5 (AMP 10) for ±1 With 1 speed only 10 of 16 are evaluable, so the counts drop. Exact counts are threshold-sensitive.
    Gait phases (walking) any speed with strides at 0.9–1.5 m/s 1 Not evaluable when no stride falls at walking speeds (BFM with 1 random speed: 78 %). SMP's double support is near 0, so its relative error is large but its absolute error is small.

    Overall pattern - Speed coverage decides the speed-relation analyses: JS, CoT and the principles. - Strides per group decide the fitted analyses: FPGD, peak ΔR² and CoT. - Threshold counts (sagittal, principles) are unstable near their thresholds whatever the sample size.

    Caveats - The study subsamples five models only. - Speeds are drawn at random, not as contiguous ranges. - The full-data values have their own sampling error. - Levels above the number of eligible speeds equal the full data.

    Minimum and recommended data (implemented: humanlike/coverage.py)¶

    Analysis Minimum (else "not evaluable") Recommended (else flagged "low")
    Feedforward JS 3 speed levels in the reference range 6 levels
    Feedback (FPGD, peak ΔR²) median 100 strides per group 250 strides per group
    Energetics (CoT) speeds from ≤ 1.0 to ≥ 3.0 m/s, 8 levels 12 levels, 10 rollouts per level
    Sagittal none (compared at matched speed) 4 levels, 20 rollouts
    Kinetic principles none (direction and shape: 0.6 m/s span, n/a below) 6 levels, 5 rollouts per level

    Speed levels - A speed level is the commanded speed when every rollout has one. - Otherwise it is the measured speed to the nearest 0.2 m/s. - The CoT's range comes from the commanded speeds. A model that was commanded to run and cannot is evaluated and fails; it is not excused.

    Where it shows - Every gated stage result carries coverage (ok, low or insufficient, with what is missing). - A stage skipped for coverage gives the reason. - evaluate(..., coverage="report") computes the analysis anyway, flagged. - The HTML report shows a "Data coverage" column, and the web app notes the low-coverage analyses. - The leaderboard row (humanlike summarize) has coverage. The leaderboard page marks low values with † and the analyses that are not evaluable with n/e.

    The manuscript models meet every minimum except KINESIS for the CoT. KINESIS was commanded to 0.5–2.3 m/s only, so its walk–run test is not evaluable. The manuscript reports FAIL; the manuscript pipeline does not apply these rules.

    In the study's counts, BFM-TS reaches only 5 speed levels inside the human range of the kinetic principles, so it would be flagged low there.

    Uncertainty and the speed-matched comparison (implemented)¶

    Bootstrap intervals (humanlike/bootstrap.py) - Every summary value carries a 95 % interval. Rollouts (or groups, for the feedback signature) are resampled 200 times with a fixed seed, so the results repeat. evaluate(..., bootstrap=0) leaves them out. - The intervals cover: - JS; - FPGD and the peak ΔR² mean; - the median z of every sagittal feature and kinetic magnitude prediction; - the count of consistent sagittal features (AMP: 6, interval 5–7, because the ankle sits at z = −2.05); - double support and the flight share. - The COT gets label_stability, the share of resamples with the same verdict (resampled within speed levels). - An interval shows how much a value would move with another draw of the same design. It does not cover a biased design: that is what the coverage rules are for.

    Stride length and frequency at matched speed - Each rollout gets a robust z against the human median and spread at its own speed (SpeedModel, as the sagittal and kinetic analyses). The result is the median z, its interval, |z| ≤ 2, and a per-speed profile (by_speed). - Unlike JS, z does not mix in which speeds the data cover, and it does not saturate. BFM-TA's JS is 1.0 bit, the maximum, but its z separates it: stride length −9.9, frequency +19.9, against AMP −1.2 and +1.5. - z still summarises only the speeds covered. A model whose deviation changes with speed gives a different median for different speed sets. In the subsampling, BFM-TA moves by 1–2 z for length and 5–10 z for frequency, while AMP moves by less than 0.5 and its verdict never flips. That movement is a property of the model, not noise, so the profile is reported alongside the median. - No speed-matched form of peak ΔR² is possible yet. The feedback reference (21 subjects walking overground at self-selected speed) has no speeds; it would need foot-placement fits per subject and speed, for example from the treadmill trials.
